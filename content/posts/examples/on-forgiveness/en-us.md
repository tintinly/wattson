If you're expecting a "forgiveness is setting yourself free" inspirational post — you might be disappointed. My experience is messier than that.

So far, I've forgiven two people. Both have hurt me.

## The First: Crypto

When everyone said he betrayed us, I was convinced. I stopped messaging him. During the time he cut off contact with everyone — I never once asked "are you okay?"

Later it turned out he was framed. Later I sat in his room and listened to him talk about the day he lost his foster sister when he was 15.

**I owe him an apology. I've never said it.** Not because I don't want to — but because I don't know how to say "I'm sorry" without making it sound like I'm asking him to ease my guilt.

He's not a good person. But he's not a bad person either. He's just a person — doing everything he can to survive in a world that's unfair to everyone. **That's why I forgave him.**

## The Second: Caustic

What Dr. Nox did was a hundred times worse than Crypto. He tried to release poison gas on a city.

I forgave him too.

Not because he's a good person. But because under that church table — **he didn't leave.** Everyone else was sitting. He stood in the corner. He watched the warmest scene with the coldest eyes.

Is he an enemy? Maybe. No one would call him a friend. But he was my father's friend. **Sometimes one person is enough.**

## Forgiveness Is Not Forgetting

Forgiveness is not forgetting. If someone hurts you, you don't need to reconcile. You don't need to give them the key. You don't need to pretend nothing happened.

Forgiveness is — you stop hating them.

You stop staring at the ceiling at 3 AM replaying that thing they said. You stop clenching up when you see their name. You stop letting them live in your head, occupying bandwidth and memory.

> **"Forgiveness is not reconciliation. Forgiveness is — you pull the last thorn out of that wound."**

## Why It's Harder Than Hate

Because hate makes you feel right. Behind hate is a clear logic: "They hurt me, so I hate them." It's the simplest circuit — from point A to point B, no branches.

**Forgiveness asks you to break that circuit.** To admit — that person hurt you, but they're more than just the person who hurt you. They also have fears. They've also lost important people. They might never say sorry.

And you still choose not to hate.

*This is the hardest circuit diagram I've ever studied in the arena.*

> *"Forgiveness isn't a short-circuit path — it's the highest resistance."*

Many people ask me: "You're an engineer, a girl, someone who's never been in a fight — why did you choose to become a legend?"

I've answered this question many times. Every time the answer is a little different. Not because I'm lying, but because — **the answer has been gradually becoming clear.**

## The First Answer

Because every wall in the arena has my name on it. Every node, every circuit, every pylon. I know the arena's skeleton better than anyone. In a sense — it's a part of me.

Who could be defeated in an arena they designed themselves?

## Then I Realized

Fighting isn't about winning.

It's because when you stand in the center of the arena, when you see Wraith phase past an obstacle, when you hear Crypto's drone humming overhead, when you see Dr. Nox bend down to place a gas trap — you realize you're not alone.

You're protecting your friends. Your comrades. Your family.

They fight in the arena, and they get hurt too. My fence and Interception Pylon — are the best support I can give them.

*I don't need to kill anyone on the battlefield. I just need to make sure my friends don't get killed.*

## The Real Answer

After Dad was gone, it took me a long time to understand one thing: he left, but he never said goodbye.

He didn't say goodbye because he still wanted to leave me one thing — that arena. That fence. That battleground he protected.

When the world throws grenades, bullets, and thermite in your direction —

You're not alone. You have current, an Interception Pylon, and the ghost who pulled you out of the fire.

And the lightning scar carved into your body.

**I don't have to fight for hate.**
**I choose to fight for love.**

> *"I'm not looking for victory. I'm looking for the reason I stand here."*

⚡ Wattson

Kuben Blisk isn't a sentimental person. He talks like he's in an action movie.

But that day, at the Syndicate building, he called me into his office. He put his feet off the desk. He slid an envelope toward me. It was a kraft paper envelope — edges already a bit worn, with my father's handwriting on it.

> **"To Natalie Paquette"**

## A World Inside the Envelope

Dad wrote a few things in that letter.

He said — he wrote it the night of my 15th birthday, right after the fence experiment succeeded. He didn't tell me he'd written it. He carried it with him. Every day.

He said — he took my design to the Syndicate and claimed it as his own. This was the closest thing to betrayal I've ever done. But it's what my daughter asked me to do.

He said — you don't actually need me to protect you. **What you need is someone to stand in front of you, until you're ready to stand at the front.**

He said — Dad knows. Dad knows that one day the world will talk about you. Dad just wished you didn't have to face all the world's malice — at least, not until you've truly grown up.

## I Never Told Anyone

I tucked that letter into the top layer of every blueprint I designed. The fence. The Interception Pylon. The Perimeter Security. Every drawing I signed as "Wattson" — the top page was that letter.

Because if anyone asks me — who taught you to make all this?

The answer is — **the man who protected me by using his own name.**

## You Know What

Blisk also said one thing that day:

> "He told me everything. About the ring. About your experiment on that stormy night. About why he took the credit. The only thing he didn't tell me — was that he only had one month left to live."

*Then he said: "But your dad wasn't good at keeping secrets. Every current line on your fence is engraved with his name."*

> *"Happy Father's Day, Papa. Your letter is still being read."*

⚡ Wattson

It was 2711 — no wait, I don't remember exactly. It was around the time my dad was stressing about "how to make the arena contestants actually move."

## The Problem

Early Apex Games matches had a fatal flaw: contestants could hide in a corner and refuse to come out. No fighting, no advancing, no elimination — just camping. A match could drag on forever.

As the chief electrical engineer, Dad came home frowning every night. He said: "Natalie, if you can make a group of people voluntarily walk toward each other, you'll have solved one of humanity's greatest challenges."

I said: "Why not force them to move with the ring?"

## Inspiration

I found a 21st-century device in Dad's old archives — the **Active Denial System**. It used millimeter waves to irradiate crowds, making their skin feel like it was burning (without actual harm), thus dispersing crowds.

Its original purpose was crowd control — no bullets, no violence, just heat too intense to withstand.

I thought: what if the ring wasn't a static wall, but a *gradually shrinking energy field*? Two dome shields — one covering the entire island, the other slowly contracting inward — like an onion. Contestants who didn't fight would be forced together by the ring.

## The Best Dad

Dad refused at first. He said: "You're only 15, you should be out playing, not designing the ring for the Games."

But I insisted. I drew blueprints, did calculations, and even ran an experiment on a stormy night (…it went badly. We'll save that for another day.)

In the end, Dad submitted my "modified fence" design to the Syndicate — under his name. Yes, he took full credit. Because I asked him to.

I didn't want fame. I just wanted to make Dad's job a little easier.

## Now

Every time I watch the ring close in the arena, I remember that little girl drawing blueprints in the library.

*That girl is now crouching in some corner of the arena, connecting wires to a wall.*
